================================================================================
CATEGORY TILE ARTWORK — IMAGE GENERATION PROMPTS
Liquor Store POS  ·  13 categories  ·  generated 2026-08-11
================================================================================

Paste these into any image generator (Midjourney, DALL·E, Firefly, Flux, SDXL).
Every prompt is written to produce ONE consistent set: same lighting, same camera,
same background, same framing. Consistency matters more than any single image —
these are seen twelve-at-once in a grid, so one odd angle or one brighter
background is immediately obvious.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SIZE AND FORMAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Generate at:   1024 × 1024 px, square (1:1)
  Ship at:       512 × 512 px PNG
  Transparency:  PNG with alpha (see section 3)

WHY SQUARE, AND WHY THOSE NUMBERS

The tile is a CSS grid cell: `minmax(170px, 1fr)` wide by 132px tall, so its
width changes with the window — roughly 170px on a small screen, up to ~300px on
a 1920px counter monitor. The image must therefore never depend on a fixed
aspect ratio.

A square source displayed with `object-fit: contain` always shows the whole
subject whatever the tile does. Nothing is ever cropped, at any window size —
which is the requirement. (`cover` would fill the tile edge-to-edge but slices
the top and bottom off a tall bottle. Do not use it here.)

512px shipped is 2× the largest realistic on-screen size (~256px), so it stays
sharp on a HiDPI screen without paying for pixels nobody sees. Generating at
1024 first gives room to re-crop or re-scale later without regenerating.

SAFE AREA — the single most important instruction

Keep the subject inside the middle 80% of the frame, with ~10% empty margin on
every side. Generators love to crop a bottle hard against the top edge; when the
tile is short and wide, `contain` shrinks the image to fit its height and an
edge-touching subject looks cramped and misaligned against its neighbours. The
margin is what makes thirteen separately-generated images read as one set.

Every prompt below already contains this instruction. Do not remove it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE STYLE PREAMBLE  (identical on every prompt — do not vary)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Professional product photograph, single centred subject, shot straight on at
  eye level, symmetrical composition. Soft diffused studio lighting from the
  upper left with a gentle rim light on the right edge. Deep matte charcoal
  background, colour #0F1117, seamless and unlit, no visible horizon or surface
  edge. Subtle warm amber highlight (#F0A830) catching the glass. Rich, moody,
  premium spirits-retail look. Subject occupies the middle 80% of the frame with
  clear empty margin on all four sides. Sharp focus, fine detail, no motion blur.
  Square 1:1 composition.

  --ar 1:1 --style raw          (Midjourney users: append this)

NEGATIVE PROMPT (paste into the negative field, or append for MJ as --no ...)

  text, letters, words, logos, brand names, labels with readable writing,
  watermark, signature, people, hands, faces, cluttered background, props,
  tabletop, wooden surface, bar counter, multiple subjects, collage, frame,
  border, drop shadow on background, white background, bright background,
  cropped subject, subject touching edge of frame, tilted angle, fisheye

READABLE BRAND NAMES: leave them out deliberately. A generated label is usually a
mangled imitation of a real brand — which is both an obvious visual defect and
somebody else's trademark on your client's screen. Blank or embossed labels look
more premium anyway.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. BACKGROUND — TRANSPARENT vs SOLID
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The prompts specify a solid #0F1117 background, which matches the app's page
background exactly and is the safe default.

BUT the recommended final asset is TRANSPARENT (cut the background out after
generating — any background remover, or generate on a flat colour and key it).

Reason: the tile is not flat. Each card is a gradient running from its own
category accent colour at 26% opacity down to #22252F at 70%:

    linear-gradient(150deg, <catColor 0.26> 0%, #22252F 70%)

A flat #0F1117 image square sitting on that gradient shows a visible rectangular
seam — a darker box floating on a lighter, tinted card. Transparent artwork sits
directly on the gradient and always matches, on every category, and keeps
matching if the theme is ever retuned.

If you would rather not cut out the background, use #1A1C25 (the panel colour)
rather than #0F1117 — it sits closer to the middle of the gradient and the seam
is far less obvious.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. FILE NAMING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Name each file with the lowercased category, so the code can resolve it from the
category string with no lookup table to keep in sync:

  whiskey.png   vodka.png     gin.png        rum.png       tequila.png
  brandy.png    arak.png      liqueur.png    wine.png      beer.png
  champagne.png mixers.png    accessories.png

Put them in:  src/assets/categories/

A category with no matching file must fall back to the existing coloured letter
badge rather than showing a broken image — the category list is user-editable, so
new categories WILL appear with no artwork. That fallback is required, not
optional.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. ONE THING TO DECIDE BEFORE GENERATING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The tile accent colours are NOT drink colours. They are generated by hashing the
category name (`catHue` in src/lib.js), so today they land like this:

    Whiskey      #51D67E   green
    Vodka        #D651A8   pink
    Gin          #D651B3   pink
    Rum          #82D651   lime
    Tequila      #51CBD6   cyan
    Brandy       #5182D6   blue
    Arak         #D651A3   pink
    Liqueur      #61D651   green
    Wine         #51BAD6   cyan
    Beer         #63D651   green
    Champagne    #D6D651   yellow
    Mixers       #5651D6   indigo
    Accessories  #51D651   green

Green whiskey, cyan wine, pink gin. It is stable and it guarantees every category
gets a distinct colour with no configuration — but once there is real photography
on the tile, a green wash behind an amber whisky bottle will look wrong.

Two ways to go, and it is worth choosing BEFORE generating 13 images:

  (a) Keep the hashed colours. Then generate the artwork on transparent
      background and keep the subject fairly neutral, so the tile tint reads as
      decoration rather than as a claim about the drink.

  (b) Replace the hash with a fixed per-category palette keyed to the real drink
      (whiskey amber, wine burgundy, beer gold, vodka ice-blue …), falling back
      to the hash for any category not in the table. The tile tint then reinforces
      the photograph instead of fighting it. This is a small change to
      src/lib.js and is what I would recommend if you are adding photography.
